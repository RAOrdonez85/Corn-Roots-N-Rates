Corn Roots by N Rate

Built from real root data measured in the field over three seasons (2017, 2018 and 2019): 153 soil cores under three N rates with three replicates per sampling, combined into one calibrated model of root development. Each panel is one 75 cm row width, 0–180 cm deep, with a corn plant in the middle. Roots fill the whole soil profile: the amount of root in each depth layer follows the root length density for that N rate (number at the right edge, cm/cm³), and line thickness follows specific root length (finer roots are thinner). Roots from the plant and the two neighboring rows meet between rows by 40 days after planting. Drag the slider or press Play.

How the roots are built

Field data. Real root measurements from 153 soil cores collected in the field: 2017 (9 sampling dates, Jun 28 – Oct 3), 2018 (3 dates, Jun 17 – Jul 26) and 2019 (5 dates, Jul 8 – Aug 20), each with 3 N rates × 3 replicates, in layers from 0–15 to 180–210 cm.

Three-season calibration. Each season's sampling days are scaled so that R2 falls on the same day (day 89, the three-season mean; actual R2 was 103, 79 and 86 days after planting). One model per N rate is then fitted to all cores from the three seasons together: the seasonal course of root mass (R² 0.67 for N0, 0.69 for N150, 0.74 for N300), converted to root length with each N rate's pooled length-to-mass ratio, and distributed with depth using the pooled share of roots in each layer before and after R2.

Rooting depth. Depth increases through the vegetative stages, reaches the maximum rooting depth at R2, and is maintained after that: 155 cm for N0, 160 cm for N150 and 168 cm for N300. The shape of the increase is fitted to the core depths before R2.

Root fineness. Line thickness in each layer follows the specific root length (mm mg⁻¹) pooled over all cores: a higher SRL means thinner, finer roots.

Root distribution. In each depth layer, lateral roots from the plant and its two neighboring rows are added across the full 75 cm width until the drawn root length matches the root length density for that N rate. The scale is the same for all three panels.

Architecture. The seed sits at 5 cm with one primary and four seminal roots. Five crown whorls between 4.6 and 1.8 cm depth produce 40 nodal roots (4 to 12 per whorl, later whorls steeper). Three whorls of brace roots (9–12 each) emerge from the first above-ground nodes from about 48 days after planting and arch down into the soil. All roots bend downward under gravity and carry lateral roots.

Shoot. Plant height, stalk diameter and leaf number grow with time; lower leaves dry from about 55 days after planting and the whole plant senesces toward the end of the season (day 140).

Spread. Lateral spread reaches the middle between rows (37.5 cm each side) at 40 days after planting, as observed.

Color. Young roots are pale and older roots darken.
