Corn Roots by N Rate

Built from real root data of conventional tall corn measured in the field over three seasons (2017, 2018 and 2019): 153 soil cores under three N rates (0, 168 and 336 kg N ha⁻¹) with three replicates per sampling, combined into one calibrated model of root development. Each panel shows two corn rows 75 cm apart, the whole plant above ground and the soil to 180 cm. Roots fill the whole soil profile: the amount of root in each depth layer follows the root length density for that N rate (number at the right edge, cm/cm³), and line thickness follows specific root length (finer roots are thinner). Roots from the two rows (and the next row on each side) meet between rows at the time the model predicts for each N rate (40 days is shown as a reference). Drag the slider or press Play.

Simulated

Everything shown here is simulated. The plants, roots and values are produced by a model calibrated against real field measurements (153 soil cores, three seasons, three N rates). The model reproduces the measured patterns of root growth over the season and with depth, but it is not a photograph or a plot of individual samples. The measured field means appear as points on the root mass chart.

How the roots are built

Field data. Real root measurements from 153 soil cores collected in the field: 2017 (9 sampling dates, Jun 28 – Oct 3), 2018 (3 dates, Jun 17 – Jul 26) and 2019 (5 dates, Jul 8 – Aug 20), each with 3 N rates × 3 replicates, in layers from 0–15 to 180–210 cm.

Three-season calibration. Each season's sampling days are scaled so that R2 falls on the same day (day 89, the three-season mean; actual R2 was 103, 79 and 86 days after planting). One model per N rate is then fitted to all cores from the three seasons together. Root mass rises until accumulation stops between R1.5 and R2 (day 86) and is maintained after that at the mean of the 30 cores sampled later in each N rate: 1,414 kg ha⁻¹ for N0, 1,950 for N168 and 1,901 for N336. The shape of the rise is fitted to the earlier cores (R² over all cores 0.64, 0.69 and 0.73). Root mass is converted to root length with each N rate's pooled length-to-mass ratio and distributed with depth using the pooled share of roots in each layer before and after R2.

Rooting depth. Roots deepen in two linear phases, using the root front velocities reported for maize in Iowa: 0.62 cm d⁻¹ until V5 (day 25), then 3.12 cm d⁻¹ until the maximum rooting depth is reached, after which depth is maintained. Maximum depth is 155 cm for N0 (reached on day 70), 160 cm for N168 (day 71) and 168 cm for N336 (day 74), around V18–VT.

Root fineness. Line thickness in each layer follows the specific root length (mm mg⁻¹) pooled over all cores: a higher SRL means thinner, finer roots.

Root distribution. In each depth layer, lateral roots from the two rows in the panel and the next row on each side are added across the full 150 cm width until the drawn root length matches the root length density for that N rate. The scale is the same for all three panels.

Architecture. The seed sits at 5 cm with one primary and four seminal roots. Five crown whorls between 4.6 and 1.8 cm depth produce 40 nodal roots (4 to 12 per whorl, later whorls steeper). Three whorls of brace roots (9–12 each) emerge from the first above-ground nodes from about 48 days after planting and arch down into the soil. All roots bend downward under gravity. Laterals curve and sag, carry short second-order branches, and grow out over about a week; growing tips show a pale root cap with root hairs just behind it.

Shoot. A conventional tall corn hybrid with 19 leaves: internodes elongate one after another, reaching the measured plant height to the top collar around R1.5: 210 cm without N (N0) and 230 cm with N (N168 and N336). Without N, plants are also drawn with the typical signs of N deficiency: paler green leaves, V-shaped yellowing that starts at the tip and runs down the midrib of the lower leaves, earlier drying of the lower leaves, a slightly thinner stalk and a smaller ear (about 15% shorter). These N-deficiency features are illustrative, not measured. Silking (R1) is on day 77 with N and 5 days later, on day 82, without N, so the N0 ear and silks lag behind while the tassel sheds pollen at the same time. Leaves have a pinched collar, wavy margins, a pale midrib and sheaths around the stalk; the stalk tapers and zig-zags at the nodes. The tassel emerges and sheds pollen around flowering, and the ear grows on its shank with silks from R1. Lower leaves dry from about 48 days after planting, and the whole plant senesces from the top during grain fill to day 140.

Row closure. Lateral spread is driven by the root length in the topsoil (0–30 cm), where the shallow nodal roots grow toward the middle between rows. Rows close when an N rate's topsoil root length reaches the three-rate mean at the 40-day reference, so rates that build topsoil roots faster close sooner. The predicted closure day for each N rate is shown under each panel and marked ▼ on the root mass chart.

Color. Young roots are pale and older roots darken.
